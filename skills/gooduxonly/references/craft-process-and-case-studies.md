## 1. Point of view over AI-default design
- **"Zombie UI":** interfaces that all look the same regardless of brand or business (Case Study Club
  No. 018, summarizing Stripe's Katie Dill). AI tends to output the most probable, already-popular
  answer, so unguided it produces generic UI. Fix: set a point of view before generating anything.
  https://www.casestudy.club/
- **Built ≠ done ≠ good.** AI makes things look finished early (rounded corners, shadows) before
  anyone asked whether they solve the problem. When building is cheap, the filter moves after the
  build — someone must play editor/design director and say no.
- **When everyone can build, differentiation gets harder and more important.**
- **Ask what to remove** before asking what to add. https://www.casestudy.club/issues/016/
- **The last 10%** — polish and detail are where craft shows. https://www.casestudy.club/issues/006/
- **When "decent design" is the default**, taste and judgement are the differentiators.
  https://www.uxtools.co/blog/what-happens-when-decent-design-is-the-default
- **Taste is trainable** through deliberate exposure and critique. https://www.uxtools.co/blog/i-was-wrong-about-taste

Apply in critiques: if a design is clean but generic, say so and name what would make it specific
to this brand/user (voice, imagery, layout rhythm, signature interaction) — without hurting usability.

## 2. Modern workflow & prototyping
- Prototyping has moved toward code: designers increasingly prototype in real code / AI coding tools
  rather than static frames. https://www.uxtools.co/blog/how-prototyping-has-changed ·
  https://www.uxtools.co/blog/this-is-the-state-of-prototyping-in-2026
- Designers who build ("vibe code") report higher satisfaction; code prototypes surface real states
  and edge cases early. https://www.uxtools.co/blog/designers-who-vibe-code-are-happier-at-work
- Research has to keep pace with faster build teams — lightweight, continuous testing.
  https://www.uxtools.co/blog/how-research-teams-are-keeping-up-with-build-teams
- **Usability testing is having a comeback** because cheap building creates more things that need
  validating. https://www.casestudy.club/issues/001/
- Adaptive/just-in-time interfaces that rearrange per user are emerging — keep predictability and
  user control (Jakob's Law, H3, H4). https://www.uxtools.co/blog/interfaces-that-rearrange-for-each-user
- Motion is now accessible to everyone — use it for meaning (state change, hierarchy, feedback), not
  decoration; respect reduced-motion. https://www.uxtools.co/blog/anyone-can-animate-now
- Write decisions down — a written source of truth keeps teams and AI tools aligned.
  https://www.casestudy.club/issues/011/ · https://www.casestudy.club/issues/003/
- Practice: UX Tools challenges for exercises. https://www.uxtools.co/challenges
- Tool comparisons: https://www.uxtools.co/tools/design

## 3. Process checkpoints
1. **Frame**: problem statement, user, job-to-be-done, success metric, constraints.
2. **Diverge**: How-might-we questions, several directions, references from real products.
3. **Converge**: pick with explicit criteria; write down why.
4. **Prototype at the right fidelity**: low for structure, code/high for interaction and states.
5. **Test** with ~5 users per round; fix; retest.
6. **Ship & measure**: activation, task success, time-on-task, error rate, retention, support tickets.
7. **Review the last 10%**: states, copy, motion, accessibility, responsive breakpoints.

## 4. Writing a UX case study
Hiring managers and clients skim. Structure for scanning, lead with outcomes.

**One-line summary per project** (Case Study Club highlights portfolios that give each project one
line and one result): "[Product]: [what you did] → [measurable result]."

**Full case study outline**
1. **Hook** — the result and the product in one sentence + hero visual.
2. **Context** — company, users, your role, team, timeline. Be honest about what was and wasn't yours.
3. **Problem** — evidence the problem was real (data, research quotes, support tickets).
4. **Constraints** — tech, business, time, legacy users.
5. **Process highlights** — only the decisions that mattered; show alternatives considered and why
   you rejected them. Name the principle or research behind each decision.
6. **Solution** — key screens with annotations, not a dump of every frame.
7. **Outcome** — metrics (conversion, activation, time saved, adoption), qualitative feedback.
   If no numbers, use usability-test results or adoption signals.
8. **Reflection** — what you'd do differently, what you learned.

Avoid: process theater (every double-diamond stage shown generically), walls of text, mockups with no
reasoning, claiming team work as solo work.
