## 1. The decision cycle
Growth.Design organizes ~100 biases around what users do with every interaction (from Buster Benson's
Cognitive Bias Codex):
1. **Information** — they filter most of what they see.
2. **Meaning** — they fill gaps with assumptions and stories.
3. **Time** — they're busy, so they take shortcuts and jump to conclusions.
4. **Memory** — they keep only fragments, and some fragments win over others.

Audit any flow by asking at each step: What will they *notice*? What will they *conclude*? What will
they *do fast*? What will they *remember*?

## 2. Key principles

### Information (what gets noticed)
- **Hick's Law** — more options, slower decisions → curate and recommend.
- **Cognitive Load** — total mental effort → cut steps, fields, jargon.
- **Progressive Disclosure** — introduce advanced features later → basic first, reveal on demand.
- **Anchoring** — the first number/option seen sets the reference → show annual savings vs. monthly,
  show the original price first. Ethically: the anchor must be real.
- **Framing** — same facts, different presentation, different decision → "90% success" vs "10% failure".
- **Priming** — earlier stimuli shape later choices → onboarding that frames the product's value.
- **Nudge** — small cues steer behavior → defaults, highlighted recommended option.
- **Decoy Effect** — an inferior option makes the target look better → classic 3-tier pricing.
- **Fitts's Law**, **Banner Blindness** (people ignore what looks like ads), **Von Restorff**,
  **Visual Hierarchy**, **Signifiers**, **Centre-Stage Effect** (people lean toward the middle option),
  **Tesler's Law**, **Feedback Loop**, **Feedforward** (tell users what will happen before they act).

### Meaning (what gets concluded)
- **Social Proof** — people copy others → reviews, user counts, logos, "popular" tags. Must be real and specific.
- **Scarcity** — limited things feel more valuable → only when true (real stock, real deadlines).
- **Curiosity Gap** — a desire to close missing information → teasers, previews, locked-content peeks.
- **Mental Model / Familiarity Bias** — people prefer what matches their expectations.
- **Reciprocity** — give first (free value, templates, trial) and people want to give back.
- **Aha! Moment** — the moment new users first feel the value → the target of all onboarding.
- **Goal-Gradient** — motivation rises near the finish → progress bars, head starts.
- **Variable Reward** — unexpected rewards engage → use for delight, not compulsion.
- **Authority Bias**, **Halo Effect**, **Noble Edge Effect** (preference for responsible companies),
  **Singularity Effect** (one person's story beats statistics), **Curse of Knowledge** (designers
  forget what novices don't know), **Survey Bias** (people answer what sounds acceptable).

### Time (shortcuts users take)
- **Default Bias** — people stick with defaults → make the default the best choice for the user.
- **Loss Aversion** — losses hurt more than equal gains please → "keep your progress" beats "get more".
- **Commitment & Consistency** — small first commitments lead to bigger ones → micro-yes first step.
- **Investment Loops / IKEA Effect / Endowment** — users value what they've built or customized →
  let them create something early.
- **Sunk Cost** — reluctance to abandon investment → don't weaponize it to trap users.
- **Labor Illusion** — visible effort increases perceived value → show the work ("Searching 300
  airlines…"), but faster isn't always better.
- **Reactance** — forced behavior is resisted → offer choice, avoid pushiness.
- **Temptation Bundling**, **Discoverability**, **Second-Order Effects** (consequences of the
  consequences), **Decision Fatigue**, **Hyperbolic Discounting** (now beats later),
  **Fresh Start Effect** (new week/month/year triggers action), **Planning Fallacy**.

### Memory (what sticks)
- **Peak-End Rule** — design the high point and the ending.
- **Zeigarnik Effect** — incomplete tasks nag → checklists.
- **Delighters** — small unexpected pleasures are remembered (micro-animations, playful copy).
- **Chunking**, **Recognition over Recall**, **Picture Superiority**, **Storytelling Effect**,
  **Negativity Bias** (one bad moment outweighs several good), **Serial Position**.
- **Provide Exit Points** — tell users when they're done for now; respectful stopping points build
  long-term trust.
- **Internal Trigger** — habits form when a feeling or situation prompts use without an external cue.

## 3. Ethics guardrails
Growth.Design repeatedly shows ethical versions beat manipulative ones over time. Apply this test to
every persuasive technique:
1. **Is it true?** (real scarcity, real reviews, real savings)
2. **Does it help the user decide in their own interest?**
3. **Would the user be upset if they understood how it worked?**
4. **Is it easy to reverse?** (cancel, undo, opt out as easy as opt in)

Refuse/flag dark patterns: fake timers or stock counts, hidden fees revealed at checkout, pre-checked
add-ons, confirm-shaming ("No thanks, I hate saving money"), roach-motel cancellation, disguised ads,
forced continuity without reminders, nagging permission prompts, infinite-scroll traps aimed at
vulnerable users. Offer the honest alternative instead (e.g., trial-end reminder emails increase trust
and can still convert).
